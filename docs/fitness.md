# Fitness functions

optimiCe runs its algorithms against a catalog of standard benchmark functions, chosen with the `fitness` field. Every function is defined for minimization: lower is better. With `direction = "maximize"` the same function is maximized instead.

Some functions only exist in a fixed number of dimensions, and some are only meaningful over a bounded region: a run's `n_dims` must fall within a function's limits, and its domain, `domain_min` to `domain_max`, must lie inside the function's domain. Both are checked at startup. A function whose domain is "any" accepts an infinite domain.

## Evaluations

Every run counts its fitness evaluations, and the count is reported with its result, in the terminal and in the [results file](output.md). Evaluations are the usual cost measure for comparing metaheuristics: an iteration costs a different number of evaluations in each algorithm and at each population size, so comparing iteration counts compares unlike things.

The count is the run's real cost because of two rules:

- **The only way to reach a fitness function is through the counted path.** No algorithm can call a function without the call being counted.
- **Every candidate solution is evaluated exactly once, when it's created.** An algorithm keeps the fitness of what it has already evaluated and never evaluates a solution again, nor evaluates one just to print it.

## sphere

The sum of squares. Unimodal, smooth and separable: the easiest function in the catalog, and the usual baseline. Defined everywhere, so it can be searched without bounds.

$$f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2$$

## rosenbrock

A long, narrow, curved valley. Finding the valley is easy; following it to the bottom is not, because the valley floor is nearly flat along its length and steep across it. Non-separable, needs at least 2 dimensions, and defined everywhere.

$$f(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \left[ 100 \left( x_{i+1} - x_i^2 \right)^2 + (1 - x_i)^2 \right]$$

## rastrigin

A sphere with a cosine ripple added in every dimension, which covers the landscape with a regular grid of local minima. Highly multimodal but separable. Its domain is limited to [−5.12, 5.12].

$$f(x) = 10n + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left[ x_i^2 - 10 \cos(2\pi x_i) \right]$$

## griewank

A wide, shallow bowl with many small local minima from a product of cosines. The product couples the dimensions, so the function is non-separable. Its domain is limited to [−600, 600].

$$f(x) = 1 + \frac{1}{4000} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2 - \prod_{i=1}^{n} \cos\!\left( \frac{x_i}{\sqrt{i}} \right)$$

## ackley

A nearly flat outer region dotted with small local minima, around a single deep funnel. A search that starts far out gets little guidance from the slope. Its domain is limited to [−5, 5].

$$f(x) = -20 \exp\!\left( -0.2 \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2} \right) - \exp\!\left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \cos(2\pi x_i) \right) + 20 + e$$

## eggholder

A rugged two-dimensional landscape of deep, irregular local minima with no overall basin to guide the search. Only defined in 2 dimensions, with its domain limited to [−512, 512]. With $x = (x_1, x_2)$:

$$f(x) = -(x_2 + 47) \sin\!\sqrt{\left| \frac{x_1}{2} + x_2 + 47 \right|} - x_1 \sin\!\sqrt{\left| x_1 - (x_2 + 47) \right|}$$

## schwefel

Multimodal and deceptive: its good regions are far apart, so a search drawn into one of them can be far from the others. Separable, with its domain limited to [−500, 500].

$$f(x) = 418.9828872724338 \, n - \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \sin\!\sqrt{|x_i|}$$

## schaffer_f6

Concentric rings of local minima around the center, whose ripples weaken with distance. Only defined in 2 dimensions, with its domain limited to [−100, 100]. With $r^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2$:

$$f(x) = 0.5 + \frac{\sin^2\!\sqrt{r^2} - 0.5}{\left( 1 + 0.001 \, r^2 \right)^2}$$
