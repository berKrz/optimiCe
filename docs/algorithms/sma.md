# SMA - Slime Mould Algorithm

Slime mould forages by spreading toward food, and the veins that carry more food grow thicker. The algorithm models that with a population of slimes, each a candidate position. Every iteration the slimes are ranked, and each gets weights from its rank: better slimes are weighted to reinforce their search, worse ones to weaken it. Each slime then either approaches the best position found so far, steered by two random peers, or contracts, and a small share of slimes is re-initialized at random. The range of both moves shrinks to zero over the run, shifting the search from exploration to exploitation.

optimiCe implements the SMA of Li et al. (2020). The run's result is the best position found over the whole run.

## How it works

### Initialization

Every slime starts at a position drawn uniformly from the init range, independently in each dimension, and is evaluated. The best of them is the starting best-so-far.

### Each iteration

**Weights.** The slimes are ranked by fitness, best first. Each slime's distance from this iteration's best fitness, as a share of the spread between this iteration's best and worst, sets the size of its weights: one weight per dimension, each with its own random factor. Slimes in the better half get weights above 1, slimes in the worse half get weights below 1. With an odd population, the middle slime counts as better, as in the reference implementation. Since the share lies in [0, 1], the weights stay within [1, 1.30] for the better half and [0.70, 1] for the worse half.

**Move ranges.** Two ranges shrink over the run's budget (see [Stopping](../configuration.md#stopping)): the *approach range* $a$, which shrinks from large values to 0, and the *contraction range* $b$, which shrinks linearly from 1 to 0. Under an iteration budget, both reach 0 at the budget's last iteration; under an evaluation budget, they end just above it.

**Moves.** Slimes then move one at a time, in order. Each slime:

1. With probability `z`, is re-initialized: its whole position is drawn again from the init range.
2. Otherwise, moves each dimension separately. The closer the slime's fitness is to the best fitness so far, the less likely it is to approach; for each dimension:
   - **Approach:** the coordinate moves to the best position found so far, plus a random step along the difference between two other slimes, picked at random. The first of the two is scaled by the slime's weight for that dimension, and the step by a random factor within the approach range. A slime that already moved in this iteration contributes its new position.
   - **Contract:** otherwise, the coordinate is multiplied by a random factor within the contraction range.
3. Is brought back into the domain, coordinate by coordinate, according to `bounds` (see [Configuration](../configuration.md)).
4. Is evaluated. If it beats the best so far, the best is updated at once, so the slimes after it in the same iteration already move toward the new best.

Contraction scales the coordinate itself, so it pulls slimes toward 0, not toward the best position. At the last iteration of an iteration budget the contraction range is 0, and a contracting coordinate lands exactly on 0. On benchmark functions whose optimum is at the origin, this pull helps the search; keep it in mind when comparing SMA with other algorithms on those functions.

In the paper's notation: $S(i)$ is slime $i$'s fitness, $bF$ and $wF$ this iteration's best and worst fitness, $DF$ the best fitness so far and $X_b$ where it was found, $X_A$ and $X_B$ the two random slimes, and $\pi$ the run's progress: under an iteration budget $t / T$, with $t$ the iteration counted from 1 out of $T$; under an evaluation budget, the share of the evaluations used when the iteration starts, setup's included. The weights are

$$W_{i,d} = 1 \pm r \, \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{bF - S(i)}{bF - wF} + 1\right), \qquad r \sim U[0, 1)$$

with $+$ for the better half and $-$ for the worse half. The move ranges and the approach probability are

$$a = \operatorname{arctanh}(1 - \pi), \qquad b = 1 - \pi, \qquad p = \tanh \lvert S(i) - DF \rvert$$

and each dimension of a slime that isn't re-initialized moves to

$$x_d = \begin{cases} X_{b,d} + v_b \, (W_{i,d} \, X_{A,d} - X_{B,d}) & \text{with probability } p, \quad v_b \sim U[-a, a] \\ v_c \, x_d & \text{otherwise}, \quad v_c \sim U[-b, b] \end{cases}$$

## Options

### Random re-initialization

`z` is the probability that a slime is re-initialized instead of moving, decided once per slime per iteration. A re-initialized slime is drawn from the init range, like the initial population. It keeps some exploration alive late in the run, when both move ranges are small. `z = 0.03`, the default, is the value used by Li et al. (2020); `z = 0` turns re-initialization off.

Checked at startup:

- `pop_size` must be positive.
- `z` must be in [0.0, 1.0].
- `bounds = "random"` needs a domain that is finite, or infinite at both ends.

## Example

On 30-dimensional Rastrigin:

```toml
[common]
fitness    = "rastrigin"
n_dims     = 30
domain_min = -5.12
domain_max = 5.12
iterations = 1000
replicates = 30

[[run]]
algorithm = "sma"
pop_size  = 30
z         = 0.03
bounds    = "clamp"
```
